# 5.7 — Documentation and training

**Parent:** [§5.7](../phase-5-hardening-pilot.md) · **Depends on:** 5.3–5.5 ·
**Unblocks:** 5.8
**Estimate:** 4 d · **Risk:** medium — the phase's most deferrable work and the
one the pilot most depends on

## Goal

Everything a person needs to run this without the developer: fourteen runbooks, a
card at the counter, an administrator guide, and a training session that actually
happened.

## Design notes

**A runbook is written when its subject is built, not in a documentation sprint.**
The deploy and rollback pages belong to [5.3e](5.3-deployment.md); restore belongs
to [5.4c](5.4-backup-and-recovery.md); the reconciliation and retention pages
belong to [5.5e](5.5-observability.md). This sub-phase is therefore a **collection
and gap-filling** exercise, and its estimate assumes that. If it turns into the
phase where all fourteen get written from scratch, the pilot slips.

**The count is fourteen, not ten.** The parent says "all ten runbooks";
[09](../../09-security-privacy-ops.md) lists fourteen and the fourteen are the
right list — they were chosen from what will actually happen. This breakdown
follows the fourteen and treats the parent's "ten" as a stale count.

**Write for the person who will be holding the page at 08:30.** Not the author,
not a developer. Each page: the symptom as the counter would describe it, the
check, the fix, and when to escalate to whom. `docs/runbooks/kiosk-ipad-setup.md`
is the shape to follow — it is the only one that exists today.

**The counter card is one laminated page and it earns every line.** Borrow,
return, scanner failed, **who to see about a card**, and **how to fill in a
register row** — with "copy the asset tag from the label" called out, because
that is the field that gets guessed, and a guessed asset tag is a backfill
conflict two days later.

**Training targets the one task only the administrator can do.** The kiosk teaches
itself. Registration does not, it is administrator-only by design (FR-40), and it
is on the critical path for everyone who has never had a card. That is where the
session's time goes.

## 5.7b Counter quick-guide and privacy notice · 0.5 d

### Work items
- [ ] One laminated A4 page: how to borrow, how to return, what to do if the
      scanner fails, **who to see to get a card**, **how to fill in a register
      row** — with the asset-tag instruction called out
- [ ] Legible standing up, from arm's length, in corridor lighting; no jargon and
      no screenshots of things that will change
- [ ] The printed privacy notice for the kiosk ([09](../../09-security-privacy-ops.md))
- [ ] Both reviewed by an attendant who did not write them, before printing

---

## 5.7c Administrator guide · 1 d

### Work items
- [ ] Screenshot-led walkthrough of registration, backfill, credentials,
      devices, loans, reports and settings
- [ ] The starred workflows get the most space: **register a borrower** and
      **type in a paper page**
- [ ] What the dashboard's attention strip means and what to do about each item
- [ ] Explicitly: what an administrator must *not* do — no editing audit rows, no
      sharing an account, no registering someone from a message without checking

---

## 5.7d Training · 0.5 d

### Work items
- [ ] One session for the equipment administrator and the counter attendants
- [ ] Registration is practised hands-on, on staging, until it is done unaided —
      it is the one task only they can perform
- [ ] Backfill practised on a real register page, timed, against the four-minute
      target so the pilot's expectation is set before day one
- [ ] Hand over the pilot's feedback channel and show how to raise an issue
- [ ] Record who attended and what was covered; a substitute attendant on week
      two is a foreseeable gap

### Exit criteria
- [ ] The administrator registers a borrower and types a full register page
      unaided
- [ ] Every attendant has handled a scanner failure once, deliberately

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Documentation is deferred into the pilot and never written | Most pages are written by the sub-phase that builds the subject; this unit only fills gaps |
| The quick-guide is written by someone who has never worked the counter | Reviewed by an attendant before it is printed |
| Training happens once and the trained person is on leave in week two | Attendance recorded; the guide and runbooks are written for someone who was not there |
| The runbook count drifts (ten vs fourteen) and pages go missing | The table above is the canonical list, and it is checked against the alert links |
